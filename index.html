How it works

How Teletype turns speech into polished text in your Mac apps.

A look behind the scenes at the native pipeline. Five stages prepare your audio, clean up verbal clutter, and deliver finished writing at your cursor, with on-device transcription and your choice of local or bring-your-own-key AI polish.

  1. 1

    Smart audio trimming

    On your Mac

    Prepares the recording, trimming silence and false starts at the edges.

  2. 2

    Local speech-to-text

    On your Mac

    Parakeet or Whisper turns the recording into text on your own CPU and GPU.

  3. 3

    Vocabulary & formatting

    On your Mac

    Snippets, custom words, fillers, emoji and number formatting, using your settings.

  4. 4

    Local & BYOK AI polish

    Your choice

    Your chosen model tidies the cleaned text, locally or through an endpoint you own.

  5. 5

    Direct insertion

    On your Mac

    Finished text goes into your app, with a clipboard fallback when needed.

Your dictation audio never leaves your Mac.

Choose a local polish model to keep the text there too. With a cloud endpoint, text goes straight to that provider under your own key. Teletype has no server to send it to, and no telemetry to send it with.

Your Mac. Your choice. Local speech engines.

On-device models

Local processing. Your audio and your text stay on your Mac.

The cleanup engine

Built to handle nuances, stumbles, and everyday formatting.

Real speech is full of pauses, false starts, corrections and spoken lists. Deterministic cleanup runs on every take, before any model reads the text. Optional AI polish handles the parts that need judgement.

A personal project

More than a side project,
less than a company.

Teletype started with my own typing bottleneck. I wanted to think out loud and have clean text appear where I was already working, without sending my voice to someone else's server to make that happen.

So it is built the way I wanted it: on-device speech recognition, deterministic cleanup, optional local polish, a plain-text archive I control, and no account to create. It is open source because a privacy claim you cannot audit is just a slogan.

I use it every day. If it gives you a little more room to think, it has done its job.
